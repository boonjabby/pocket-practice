    Optional: measure the playing area

    What the diagrams tell you

    Divide the playing width into four and the length into eight. Ball positions refer to their centres, measured from the top-left cushion corner. If there are no diamonds, use an imaginary grid or measured reference points along the rails.

    The blue area is a target you judge on your real table. These are setup diagrams, not simulated trajectories. Exact jaw dimensions, cloth speed, nap and cushion response are not measured.

    Review this shot

    Thick = undercut: the object ball did not cut enough. Thin = overcut: it cut too far. Use the shot direction, not screen left/right.

    Add pace & spin for more useful guidance

    Best estimates are enough. Tip position does not tell us the spin left at contact. Leave it “Not sure” if you could not observe it.

    Understand throw

    On a cut, friction between the balls can turn the object ball away from the pure geometric line. With no side spin, this usually reduces the cut angle: an undercut.

    Slow stun around a half-ball hit deserves particular attention. There is no universal millimetre aiming correction: shot angle, speed, spin and ball conditions matter.

    Side spin also introduces spin-induced throw, deflection and swerve. Adding outside spin is not an automatic cure.
